The First Emperor

The Origin of Everything

Before the Muqadas were the Muqadas, there was a man. His name is not recorded anywhere that still exists. He was the first of his people — not a spacefaring civilization yet, just a planet running out of everything. He was extraordinarily intelligent, possessed a will that no one around him could fully explain, and under his leadership his people reached heights no other civilization in their region of existence had touched.

Then the resources ran out.

He spent one hundred years walking — crossing dead worlds, empty systems, the cold between stars — looking for something that could save what remained of his people. He found nothing. One by one, they died. All of them. By the time he reached the very last second of his own life, his brain already failing, his body already going, he was completely alone.

And then he looked up.

What he saw has no name. It should not have been visible to any living thing. In that final fraction of a second — with his mind breaking down, all filters gone, all limits dissolved — the universe opened. Every piece of information that had ever existed, was existing, or would ever exist flooded into him simultaneously. The complete record of all things, in an instant.

He did not die. Instead — he made a chant. The words came from somewhere deeper than thought, shaped by everything he had just seen. The chant made him immortal. Not gifted immortality, not inherited — made. He wrote the rules of his own existence into the fabric of what is.

He came back. He rebuilt. He created the Muqadas — the hand system, the Number inheritance, all of it — from the knowledge of what he had seen. He built the Tree to hold that knowledge, passing it from Emperor to Emperor so it would never be lost again.

What He Saw — And What It Cost

Whatever he saw that day was not meant to be seen. A god — unnamed, nature and origin unknown — noticed. And it decided that what he knew had to be destroyed. Not him, not his people. The information. The knowledge stored in the Tree.

The Wipe was not random. The faction wars, the bio-weapons, the cascading disasters — they were the visible layer. Underneath all of it was a single directive: destroy the Muqadas, destroy the Tree, destroy what the first Emperor saw. The last move in that campaign was a messenger who found Lezmyon and told him to burn the Tree.

The Rebirth — And The Empty Vessel

Adham is the first Emperor, reborn. That is why he was born with more than one hand — the race's own system, built by the first Emperor himself, identified him when he arrived. The Numbers moved toward him not by chance but by something written into the design from the beginning.

He does not know any of this.

The first Emperor stored everything he knew in the Tree — every memory, every truth, every fragment of what he saw at the edge of death. The Tree burned. The memories burned with it. Adham carries the soul of the first Emperor and none of his knowledge. He is the vessel without the contents. The crown without the history.

The god that sent the messenger does not know Adham exists. It believes the information is gone. It believes it won.

The Muqadas

The Muqadas were an ancient spacefaring race built around a single religious truth: their god reached down and assigned each of them one of his one trillion hands. Every Muqadas was born with a Number — from 1 to 1,000,000,000,000 — and a unique power tied to that number.

When a Muqadas dies, their Number passes to a random living member of the race, along with an additional hand. No choosing, no bloodline, no destiny. The hands fall where they fall. The one with the most hands becomes Emperor — a title carrying a divine lock: immortality. The Emperor cannot die.

Except Adham. There is no one left to be born. There is no one left to die and scatter their hands elsewhere. Every Number has drifted to him, and the tide has nowhere left to go.

Adham's Birth — Destined Emperor

When Adham was born, something unprecedented happened. For the first time in Muqadas history, a newborn took more than one hand at birth — pulling the consolidated 999,999,999,996 extinct Numbers that had naturally funneled into his surviving family members during the Wipe. His parents knew immediately: he was destined to be Emperor. Not by chance — written into the race's design from the beginning. They said nothing. He was a child, in hiding. They would tell him at twenty, as tradition demanded. They ran out of time.

What Adham Doesn't Know

He is the last Muqadas alive. He was always going to be Emperor. His birth announced it. The Emperor is immortal. He has been dying and surviving for years and assumes he is simply lucky.

The Wipe

A trillion people don't die from one thing. They die from everything going wrong at once.

When the Tree's mission became known — a cosmic force intending to give every universe a genuine chance at a better life — not everyone welcomed it. Beneath the visible wars and bio-weapons was a single directive from an unnamed god: destroy the Muqadas, destroy the Tree, destroy what the first Emperor saw.

The decisive blow was biological: weapons engineered specifically around Muqadas physiology, spreading in waves through a civilization already destabilized by conflict. A cure was developed and never distributed. A ceasefire collapsed days before a critical resupply. A migration of survivors reached a contaminated system.

Not conspiracy on the surface. Just the universe failing to leave enough space between disasters for anyone to breathe. But underneath — a god was running the board.

Of a trillion people, four survived. A family — mother, father, brother, and sister. They fled to Earth and settled in Egypt. Adham was born there. They tried to give him something ordinary before they could give him the truth. They ran out of time for the truth.

Powers & Abilities

Adham's full power set is theoretically limitless — one trillion unique abilities inherited from a dead race. He operates on instinct. He is an ocean who thinks he's a glass of water.

Fighting Style

Adham doesn't rush. He doesn't need to. He's immortal — and he uses that fact like a weapon. His approach to combat is built on a single principle: let them show you everything first.

He takes hits. Studies them. Reads your rhythm, your tells, the way you shift weight before a swing. He watches the way a scientist watches — cataloguing every move you make while his body absorbs punishment that would end anyone else. He is patient in a way that becomes terrifying once you realize what the patience is for.

Then he hits back. Your own moves, returned with perfect form — or his own style, built specifically to counter everything you just showed him. Every fight is a study session where he is the student and the exam at the same time. By the time he starts fighting back, he already knows how it ends.

The Signature Hand

If he takes a hit from any attack and survives, he can replicate that exact attack once — same type, same power level. He thinks of it as his only real ability. It is one of a trillion.

Golden Blood

There was a period in Muqadas history where no rain fell for four hundred years. The entire civilization prayed for four centuries without answer. Then a golden rain fell from the sky. One drop landed in the Emperor's cup. He drank it. From that moment, Golden Blood ran in his veins — passed through generations, becoming the foundation of Muqadas biology.

Runs in Acts 1 through 100, scaling exponentially. Each new tier is categorically different from the last. The passive side amplifies all stats. The active side unlocks creation and regeneration abilities. Golden Blood cannot be weaponized to harm directly. The deeper someone goes into its Acts, the stronger their biological pull toward doing good becomes. Not compulsion — pressure.

Act 101 — The Threshold

Unlocked only at the edge of death, only when the person is genuinely willing to give everything for someone else — not survival, not revenge. Emotional condition, not physical. Every Act 101 is unique to the person.

Adham has used Act 101 at least once before during his multi-universe travels. The memory was lost when the rebirth arrow glitched his existence.

Adham's Act 101 — "The Last Emperor"

He becomes a temporary conduit for every Muqadas who ever lived. The trillion dead speak through him — as pure restoration. He can fully heal or pull back from death's edge any number of people simultaneously. He cannot use it on himself. He cannot weaponize it. The cost: burns as close to empty as his body allows. Wakes up days later with nothing left. Cooldown: one use every 100 years.

The man who killed 1.5 billion people has a transcendence ability that exclusively saves lives — he bypassed the Golden Blood pull through overwhelming grief and a genuine belief, however distorted, that he was sparing those people from suffering.

Chaos Blood

Same 101 Acts, same scaling, same healing speed — everything else a mirror flipped into darkness. Heals by destroying: every cell burns and dies like cancer, replaced by new cancerous cells. Always regenerating, always in pain. Where Golden Blood creates, Chaos Blood destroys at identical scale and precision. The deeper someone progresses, the stronger their pull toward destruction becomes. Reverse-engineered by Lezmyon from studying Golden Blood.

The Emperor's Summon

Every Emperor of the Muqadas carried one secret that was never written down, never spoken aloud, never passed through the inheritance system. Adham has never been told. He does not know it exists.

The summon is a being — or a force wearing the shape of one — that exists outside the normal hierarchy of power. Gods can be killed by it. It is the single most destructive thing the Muqadas ever had access to.

When it became clear the race was going to lose, the Emperor summoned it. It cannot be controlled — this was always known. He summoned it anyway. It destroyed their enemies. It also destroyed the Muqadas. The four survivors escaped only because they were already gone before it reached them.

Fie The Wife

She was the first person Adham met in the assassin universe who talked to him like he was just a person. Not a weapon, not a rumor. Just a person who looked tired and was bad at small talk. No powers. Didn't know what he was.

What she had was steadiness. She remembered small things. She had opinions about music and shared them without being asked. She laughed at the wrong moments in films and didn't apologize for it. She made the apartment feel like somewhere worth coming back to.

The Memory He Keeps

A Tuesday. She is sitting on the kitchen counter — "chairs are for guests and I live here" — talking about something he has already forgotten the subject of. He is not really listening. He is just watching her talk, thinking: this is the world. This right here. Whatever else exists, this is the part that counts. He has never told anyone about that moment.

The End

She left. What matters is that the steadiness turned out to be a surface, and underneath it was something he hadn't seen coming. He did not fall apart. He burned. 1.5 billion people became the distance between who he was on that Tuesday in the kitchen and who he was capable of being when the last soft thing he owned was taken. He does not blame her anymore. He doesn't have the energy. What remains is not anger — just the absence of the thing that used to be there, which is its own kind of permanent.

Balance's Test

The Rules

Balance selects a chosen individual and sends them an Assistant — a version of that person in the opposite gender. The Assistant is real: their own consciousness, their own powers, their own presence. They know they are going to die — and that it will be at the hands of the chosen one. What they choose to do with that knowledge is entirely their own. At the end of the test, the chosen one must kill the Assistant. No negotiation. No alternative.

World Mechanics

Jabrial — Nature & Resurrection

Jabrial is not a singular being that can be permanently destroyed. He is an angel made of light. As long as Seraph is alive, Seraph can fire an arrow of light and wish it to become Jabrial — instantly reforming him. Killing a physical instance of Jabrial eliminates that manifestation only.

Fresh — Domain Limits

Fresh is God of Time, but only within the domain of the Nine's universe. Outside it, his dominion over time does not follow him. In the Refugee Universe, time is governed by Balance through a physical clock mechanism. Fresh is subject to the same physical limitations as anyone else there.

Personality & Psychology

Adham is not broken. Broken things don't function. He functions extremely well — professional, precise, economically motivated. He takes jobs, completes them, gets paid.

Underneath that: the settled kind of pain, the kind that becomes part of your structure. He likes money because money is honest. It doesn't pretend to care about him. The transaction is the whole relationship and he finds that clean.

He is not cruel — he doesn't enjoy suffering. But he has a very high threshold for what he considers necessary, and that threshold was recalibrated by grief a long time ago.

Moral Lines

Everything Adham does now runs through one filter: Linksia. He will do anything to protect her — no limit, no line, no hesitation. But the inverse is just as absolute: he will not do anything she would see as bad. He wants to look good in her eyes. Not powerful, not feared — good.

The man who burned 1.5 billion people now measures his actions against the opinion of one child. That is not weakness. That is the only anchor he has left, and he holds it with everything.

Daily Life — The Refugee Universe

In the Refugee Universe, Adham is not hunting. He's not taking contracts. He helps people at an orphanage — kids with nowhere to go, the kind of situation he recognizes without needing to say why. He also runs a restaurant. Cooking, serving, cleaning up. Ordinary work done by someone who is anything but ordinary.

It is the closest thing to a normal life he has ever built. The orphanage because he understands what it means to be a child with no one. The restaurant because it is honest work that feeds people. Both of these choices say more about who he is now than any power ever could.

Dramatic Irony

He Believes His powers came from the man who destroyed him.
The Truth They are his by blood and inheritance — the legacy of the first Emperor he is the rebirth of.
He Believes Surviving is luck.
The Truth It is divine law written into his title.
He Believes His family hated him.
The Truth They were the last people who knew what he truly was, and they died holding that secret to protect him.
He Believes He is alone.
The Truth He is a monarch — the last of a lineage a trillion souls wide, the rebirth of the man who built that lineage from nothing, now compressed into one person taking contracts because he likes the money.

The Muqadas are gone. But their Emperor is not.
And the Emperor — by every law they ever lived under —
cannot die until he chooses to understand what he is.

He has not chosen yet.
